02 · Costs

Who actually pays for integration?

Integration is not free. Participants, municipalities and taxpayers all have a role in paying for the process. But how much does it cost, and is the system fair?

What do participants have to pay?

Costs depend on a person's situation and which integration rules apply.

Course costs

Course prices vary by school. People who are not asylum status holders may have to pay themselves or may be able to borrow from DUO for an approved school.

For some asylum status holders, the first two attempts at each exam are free.

Who pays?

Participants

Depending on their route, people may pay for lessons, exam attempts and a citizenship application. These costs can add up, especially for someone on a low income.

Taxpayers

Local governments pay for integration courses for asylum status holders under the current system. This uses public money, but can support people in learning Dutch and participating in society.

Can the costs make integration harder?

The problem

If someone has a low income and must pay for lessons or retake exams, the costs may make integration harder. At the same time, public support for courses costs taxpayers money.

Our recommendation

Keep support for people who cannot afford the process, and make the rules clear. For example, offer extra financial help to low-income participants who do not already qualify for funded courses.

This is our recommendation, not a description of a current national rule.

How could we improve the system?

1. Clear cost overview

Give everyone a simple list of costs, what is paid for and what they need to pay themselves. This helps them plan ahead.

2. Targeted financial help

Let people with less money get help paying for courses or exam retakes if they do not already receive financial support.

3. Avoid extra costs

Give free practice materials and clear exam instructions so people know what to expect and can prepare without paying for extra help.
