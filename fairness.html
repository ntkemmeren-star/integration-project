03 · Fairness to participants

Is the system fair to everyone?

Participants are expected to complete their integration, but do they have enough influence over their own pathway? And what happens when they fail an exam or struggle financially?

The three main problems

Fairness is an important part of integration. These are the problems we identified and our proposed solutions.

Problem 01

Participants have limited influence over their pathway

In the Netherlands, newcomers who are required to complete civic integration follow an integration pathway based on their circumstances, abilities and goals.

This is mostly done by municipalities, which determine which pathway is most suitable for each participant. Although participants are involved in the process, their personal preferences, educational background, work experiences and future ambitions may not be well considered.

Participants following a pathway that does not fully match their abilities or goals can potentially reduce their motivation and the effectiveness of their integration.

Our solution

Give participants a genuine say in their own pathway

The system could be improved by giving participants a stronger voice in the decisions that are being made about their integration pathway.

Having regular meetings can allow the plan to be adjusted when needed. This makes the system more transparent and gives participants greater responsibility for their own integration.

Problem 02

Not enough feedback after failing an exam

Participants in the Dutch civic integration system pay for their own exams. When they fail the exam, they do not always get enough information to understand exactly what they need to do to improve.

Without clear feedback, it is difficult to know what to practise before taking the exam again.

Our solution

Give participants clear feedback when they fail their exam

After a participant fails an exam, DUO should provide more detailed feedback about the areas where they struggled. The feedback could explain which parts need improvement.

This can help participants prepare better and save them additional effort and money.

Problem 03

Financial differences between participants

Taking part in civic integration can involve financial struggles, especially for participants with limited income or those who are still looking for work.

Even if one part is covered, there are multiple other things people need money for. Financial differences can cause inequality and make integration harder.

Our solution

Reduce financial differences between participants

The government and municipalities should ensure that participants who face financial struggles can access appropriate assistance.

They can cover necessary expenses, such as transport and childcare. Clear rules about this and better communication about available support would help participants understand what they are entitled to receive.

Having this kind of help can reduce stress and make their integration easier.

What would a fairer system look like?

More control

Participants have a stronger voice in their own pathway.

Clear communication

People receive feedback that helps them improve.

Equal opportunities

People who struggle financially can access appropriate help.

Source: Wet inburgering 2021 and our research notes.
