01 · Effectiveness

Does the system actually work?

The Netherlands integration policy is partly successful. It helps newcomers learn Dutch and get support, but finding jobs and finishing the integration process on time is still difficult.

Goals and results

The government has several goals for integration. But achieving these goals is not always easy.

Goal 01

Learn Dutch and participate

The civic-integration system requires relevant newcomers to learn Dutch and participate in Dutch society. The newer system combines language learning with work, education or participation.

Source: Overview – Integration in the Netherlands – Inburgeren.

Goal 02

Increase employment

Employment has generally increased and unemployment and social-benefit dependence have decreased. However, people of non-European origin are still less likely to have paid work than the Dutch average.

CBS – Integration and Society 2024

Goal 03

Improve education

Educational attainment has improved among several migrant-origin groups. The second generation generally does better than migrants born abroad, although differences from the Dutch average remain.

Source: CBS – Integration and Society 2024.

Goal 04

Reduce socioeconomic differences

There has been progress, particularly among the second generation, but differences remain in income, employment, housing and reliance on benefits.

Source: CBS – Integration and Society 2024.

Goal 05

Promote participation and social cohesion

Integration is occurring, but results are uneven between origin groups and generations. The second generation tends to be closer to the Dutch average than migrants born abroad.

CBS – Report on Integration and Society 2024

How can integration be improved?

Integration in the Netherlands could be improved by offering more Dutch lessons and helping newcomers find jobs. Language lessons could be combined with work experience so people can learn Dutch while gaining useful skills.

Schools and colleges should also give newcomers more support with their education. More community activities could be organised where newcomers and Dutch people can meet.

The government could also help with finding housing and provide better support during the integration process.

Finally, discrimination in jobs and housing should be reduced. These changes could help newcomers become more independent, find work and feel more at home in the Netherlands.

Our conclusion

The integration policy has achieved some progress, especially in education and employment for the second generation. However, differences remain. This means the system is partly successful, but there is still room for improvement.
