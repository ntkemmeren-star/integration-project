04 · Ease and accessibility

Is integration actually easy to access?

Imagine moving to the Netherlands. New country, new language, new people. And on top of all that you have to learn Dutch, understand Dutch society, get a job and pass your integration exams. Sounds easy, right? Well, not exactly.

The system now

People who move to the Netherlands permanently sometimes have to complete civic integration. They learn Dutch, learn about Dutch society and participate in society.

B1 route

Language learning and participating through paid or unpaid work.

Education route

Primarily for people preparing for further education.

Z-route

For people who need more help becoming independent.

The municipality helps participants choose the right route. They follow lessons and complete the exams and other requirements for their pathway.

The problems

Problem 01

Not enough language teachers

There aren't enough teachers for everyone to help them learn Dutch because of a shortage of teachers. Because of this, a lot of people have to wait for lessons or don't get enough help.

This makes it harder to learn Dutch and finish their integration on time.

Evidence

The Netherlands Court of Audit researched civic integration in 2026. In their report Onbenut potentieel, they mention a shortage of language teachers.

Our solution

More Dutch teachers and lessons

  • Hire more Dutch teachers or volunteers.
  • Offer more classes in places where there aren't enough.
  • Provide online lessons as an extra option.
Problem 02

It's hard to combine work with lessons

Some people also have work beside their lessons, which could make it hard to attend those classes. Most lessons are during work hours, so they might clash with their work.

Evidence

The Netherlands Court of Audit found that combining paid work with compulsory language lessons can be difficult. This slows down people's progress with integration.

Our solution

More flexible lesson times

  • Offer more evening and weekend classes.
  • Let people choose lesson times that fit their schedules better.
  • Make it easier to combine work and learning Dutch.
Problem 03

Parents struggle with childcare

Parents with young children might not have anyone to look after them during lessons or work. Childcare can also be expensive and hard to arrange.

Because people can't find childcare, they may have to miss classes to look after their children.

Evidence

The Netherlands Court of Audit mentioned childcare as a problem affecting civic integration. Without childcare, some parents have fewer opportunities to attend lessons.

Our solution

Make childcare easier to arrange

  • Help parents find affordable childcare.
  • Offer childcare near lesson locations if possible.
  • Give parents more flexible lesson times.

How effective would our improvements be?

More teachers

Could help people get lessons sooner and give more people support. However, hiring and training teachers takes time and money.

Flexible lesson times

Would help people who work during the day. However, evening and weekend classes also need enough teachers.

Childcare support

Could help parents attend lessons more often. However, childcare costs money and places might be limited.

What already works?

  • People get help from their municipality.
  • There are different routes for different situations.
  • DUO provides information about exams and practice questions.

What could be better?

  • Some people struggle to find enough language lessons.
  • Work can clash with lesson times.
  • Parents might struggle to find childcare.
  • Not everyone has the same amount of time or support.

Main evidence: Netherlands Court of Audit – Onbenut potentieel (2026)
